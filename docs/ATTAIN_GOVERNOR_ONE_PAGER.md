# Iowa ATTAIN Application — One-Page Summary (Governor's Office)

**Program:** FHWA Advanced Transportation Technologies and Innovation
(ATTAIN). **Request:** $12M Federal / $3M non-Federal (20%, largely in-kind
across partner states). **Deadline:** August 11, 2026.

## What Iowa is proposing

Iowa DOT will lead a multi-state project on the I-35 corridor that brings
**public and private data together** for the two problems truck drivers and
travelers feel most: work zones that appear without warning, and nowhere to
park at the end of a shift.

**The headline goal: cut the average time a truck driver spends searching
for parking at the end of a shift from 60 minutes to 30.** National research
(ATRI driver diaries) shows drivers lose ~56 minutes and ≈$5,000 in wages a
year searching for parking — and that the search is hardest exactly when
federal hours-of-service clocks are running out.

## How it works (one paragraph)

Corridor states keep publishing exactly what they publish today — no new
systems or staff. The project layers independent, private-sector evidence on
top (vehicle probe data, construction e-ticketing, connected devices, in-cab
alert networks, camera-based AI parking counts), verifies every work zone,
predicts parking availability at every facility hourly, and delivers one
trusted feed through a single governed exchange (MITRE) to the places
drivers already look: Google, Waze, Apple, in-cab fleet systems, and highway
signs.

## Why Iowa leads credibly

- The core platform is **operational today** — ingesting ~39 work zone feeds
  nationally, with parking prediction calibrated against **Iowa's own
  rest-area camera network**.
- Iowa is the **I-35 Anchor State** under the AASHTO-coordinated corridor
  initiative, and this scope is the CCAI year-one work plan.
- Partner states join by letter of commitment — the ask of them is
  deliberately small, which is why the coalition scales.

## What Iowans (and the nation) get

| Outcome | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Parking search cut in half | Driver hours returned to the supply chain; freight moves on schedule |
| Fewer trucks on ramps & shoulders | A documented fatal-crash factor, reduced |
| Fewer hours-of-service violations | Drivers stop being forced to choose between compliance and safety |
| Work zones visible in every nav app | Fewer rear-end crashes in queues; workers safer |
| Public accountability | Every state gets a monthly data quality report card on a public dashboard |

## Timeline & accountability

36 months. Performance measured from day one by instruments that already
exist — parking prediction accuracy tracking and independent work zone
verification — reported publicly, so FHWA and the Governor's Office can
watch the corridor improve quarter by quarter.
